All of them in one window, in a list, each named after the thing you actually asked for. A dot beside each says whether it is working, waiting, or has stopped with an error. You never have to guess which window was which.

You walk away. Your Mac sleeps. The agent stops.

An agent working through a real task takes minutes, sometimes longer. So you go and do something else. Your display sleeps on the usual timer, the machine idles down, and the run dies somewhere in the middle of it.

Ocarina holds your Mac awake for as long as it is open. It is on from the moment you launch, because the whole problem is that you were never going to remember to turn it on.

The three moments that stop a beginner cold, and what happens instead.

Make the terminal your own.

Fourteen looks, each with its own colour, texture and weight. Pick one and the whole window follows, tabs and panels included.

None of them can hide an error

Every colour in every theme is measured against its own background before the theme is offered. A palette that would make red text unreadable is listed as broken rather than quietly shipped.

contrast vs. background · 4.5:1 floor

You cannot break something by accident.

Most of what makes a terminal frightening is that it does exactly what you tell it, instantly, with no warning. Ocarina puts a sentence in the way.

⚠ This is 3 separate commands, not one.
⚠ rm -rf deletes files permanently.
⚠ sudo runs as administrator.

A risky paste is read back to you

You are following instructions from a page you cannot check. Ordinary commands paste silently. Only text that would run several things, or delete something, stops for a sentence.
